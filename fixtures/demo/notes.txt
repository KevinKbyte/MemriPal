Good morning, everyone. Today I want to show you why the memory palace still works.

The method of loci is more than two thousand years old. Greek and Roman orators used it to give long
speeches without notes.

The idea is simple: you place each thing you want to remember at a familiar spot along a route you
already know.

Our brains are built for places. Spatial memory is strong because finding food and shelter once
depended on it.

Vivid images stick better than plain facts. A strange, funny or gross picture is far easier to recall
than a list.

Engage every sense. Imagine how the scene sounds, smells, tastes and feels, not just how it looks.

Order comes for free. Walking the route in the same direction every time replays the items in the
same order.

Start small. Ten or twelve spots in one room are enough for a first palace.

Review on a schedule. Walk the palace again after a day, then after a week, so the images do not fade.

Memory champions use this exact technique to memorize decks of cards in under a minute.

Students can use it for exams, and speakers can use it to drop their slides.

So pick a room tonight, choose twelve spots, and try it on your next shopping list. Thank you.
