Retrieval practice. Close the book and write down everything you remember, then check what you missed. Pulling an idea out of memory makes it stronger than reading it again.

Spaced practice. Spread your study over days and weeks instead of cramming the night before. Short sessions with gaps between them make the learning last.

Interleaving. Switch between different topics or problem types in one session instead of doing one kind over and over. Mixing them forces you to choose the right method each time.

Elaboration. Ask yourself how and why things work, and connect each new idea to what you already know. Explaining the details in your own words builds a richer memory.

Concrete examples. Turn abstract ideas into specific, real cases you can picture. Collect several examples and notice what they have in common.

Dual coding. Combine words with visuals such as diagrams, timelines or sketches. Explaining a picture in words gives the brain two ways to find the same idea.
